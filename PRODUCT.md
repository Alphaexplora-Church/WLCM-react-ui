# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences of equal weight:

**First-time visitors** — people curious about faith or about WLCM, usually arriving through a friend's referral or social media. They're deciding whether to show up on Sunday. They need to feel welcome before they read a single word. The bar question: "Is this a place for someone like me?"

**Current congregation** — members and regular attenders across 21 churches in the Philippines. They come for practical things: service schedules, events, giving, sermons, ministry info. They need utility without losing the sense that the site belongs to their community.

Both audiences are non-negotiable. No page should feel like it excludes either.

## Product Purpose

Words of Life Christian Ministries (WLCM) is a network of 21 churches across the Philippines, established 2002, headquartered in Greenhills, San Juan. The website is the front door — it converts curious outsiders into first-time visitors and keeps the congregation connected to the life of the church. Success looks like a skeptic booking a first visit and a member finding what they need without friction.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, grounded, warm.

The emotional goal in the first five seconds: "This is a place I'd feel welcome." Not hype, not institution, not startup — a community that has substance and soul.

## Anti-references

- **Generic megachurch SaaS template** — stock photography of praying hands, gradient CTAs, "Join us this Sunday" in Proxima Nova, hero sections with faded cross imagery. Every mid-size church looks like this.
- **Startup-minimalist whitespace** — all-white backgrounds, 14px light-weight sans-serif, "We believe in community" floating in a sea of padding. Faith reduced to a brand statement.

## Design Principles

1. **Both belong here.** Every page must serve a skeptical first-timer and a long-time member without compromise. If a design choice excludes one, find a different solution.

2. **Warmth through specificity.** Generic warmth (smiling stock faces, vague copy) doesn't land. Concrete detail does — actual service times, real church locations, specific stories, real names. The interface should feel particular to WLCM, not "church in general."

3. **Cinematic confidence, not spectacle.** The dark aesthetic earns its place by feeling intentional and inviting. The moment it tips into concert-venue hype or SaaS cool, it has failed.

4. **Weight earns meaning.** Every heavy visual choice — dark backgrounds, full-screen layouts, oversized type — should carry emotional or spiritual purpose. Darkness as drama is decorative. Darkness as depth is intentional.

5. **Faith, not formula.** The design should feel unmistakably like WLCM, not like "how church websites looked in 2024." Resist the template at every turn.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum — contrast ratios, keyboard navigation, alt text on all meaningful images. The congregation spans a wide age range; legibility and tap-target sizing matter more than assumed.
